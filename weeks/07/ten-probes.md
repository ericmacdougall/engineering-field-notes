# Week 07 — ten employer experiments before changing hiring

These are tests for the **employer**, not applicant homework. The article's three-question interview is an editorial proposal. No real candidate study is reported here. Use a role-specific job analysis, consent and privacy controls for any pilot data.

1. **Work sample alignment.** Review ten recently accepted role changes. Count decisions, implementation, review, incident work and handoffs. If the interview overweights a rare task, change it.
2. **Expert review.** Ask two experienced people in the role to inspect the supplied patch and expected answer anchors independently. Record which questions reveal real job reasoning versus trivia.
3. **Delivery consistency.** Give pilot participants the same one-page artifact, diagram, three questions, late fact and time. Observe whether a delivery difference changes the evidence reviewers can score.
4. **Contract gap quality.** The fictional producer diff is green but downstream compatibility is unproved. Confirm that a strong answer has to identify a cross-system decision, not merely spot a syntax defect.
5. **Reviewer disagreement.** Score the same synthetic response independently against prewritten anchors. Use the [local calibration script](../../examples/hiring-interview-calibration/README.md) to flag missing evidence and large gaps; investigate the reason, not only the number.
6. **Late-fact revision.** Reveal that a consumer cannot upgrade for two weeks and old events may replay. Check that the score rewards a changed plan with a named owner and evidence boundary rather than first-answer confidence.
7. **Falsifying replay.** Ask what old event, consumer version and expected business result would disprove the release claim. The employer later builds and runs the fixture; the candidate does not owe code.
8. **Build versus buy.** Use the meeting-scheduler case. Make the scoring accept a justified buy, compose, build or wait answer when lifetime ownership, differentiated value and the person's actual work are addressed.
9. **Attention triage.** Add a production alert beside migration and documentation work. Check whether the response protects review capacity and asks for customer impact before multiplying agent streams.
10. **Outcome check.** Pilot the process, then compare reviewer agreement and subsequent accepted-work evidence. Revise anchors that fail to distinguish technical fundamentals from fluent process language. Do not infer predictive validity from a single synthetic run.

The [OPM structured-interview overview](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/structured-interviews/) and [guide](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/structured-interviews/guide.pdf) support consistent job-related questions and anchored scoring. They do not validate these ten specific probes or set a required question count. A separate short paid practical, if needed, is Eric's fairness recommendation.
